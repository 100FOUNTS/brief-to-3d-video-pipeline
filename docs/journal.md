## 2026-10-15 · Phase 1 (done with Sonnet 5.5, not with the local model)
- Result: `video/scene.html`, `video/render.mjs`, `make video` -> `out/title.mp4` (1920x1080, 30 fps, 240 frames, 8 s).
- Failure 1: the first MP4 came out as `yuvj420p` (full range), because the JPEG captures come in as "JPEG range". It is fixed in the FFmpeg filter (`scale=in_range=full:out_range=limited,format=yuv420p`) and by tagging bt709/tv. Without this some players show washed-out colors.

## 2026-10-15 · Phase 5: voice and music (done with Sonnet 5.5)
- Isolated environment `audio/.venv` (Python 3.12 managed by uv): `mlx-audio` 0.5.9 (MIT) + `misaki[en]` 0.9.4 (Apache 2.0; it bundles espeak-ng: Homebrew was not needed). Model Kokoro-82M-bf16 (Apache 2.0), downloaded from Hugging Face (~40 s). Kokoro has 3 Spanish voices: ef_dora, em_alex, em_santa (I used the first two).
- Voice (`audio/narration.py`): one sentence per scene (`docs/script.json` with the start and window of each). It checks that each sentence fits in its window and, if not, speeds it up in 5% steps (max 1.30×). Result: all fit; `s3-articulated` at 1.20× and `s4-warm` at 1.10×. Generating the 2 voices takes 7.6 s.
- Music (`audio/music.py`): numpy, 70 bpm, C major (Cmaj7 | Am7 | Fmaj7 | G6sus | Cmaj7 | Fmaj7), a pad with 3 detuned oscillators, an arpeggio that enters at 2.3 s and gets brighter at 13 s (when the light turns golden), a discreet kick, reverb by convolution. Deterministic (two runs byte-identical), 20 s, peak -3 dBFS.
- Mix (`audio/mix.sh`): sidechaincompress (the music drops ~14 LU while the voice speaks) + amix (voice 1.7×) + loudnorm. Result: -16.1 / -15.9 LUFS, true peak -1.5 dB.
- Verification without hearing: (1) loudness and peaks (ebur128); (2) spectrograms: the voice appears in 5 blocks that match the scene windows; (3) `audio/measure_mix.py`: the voice sits +12.1 to +19.3 LU above the music in the speech windows (rule of thumb >= 10); (4) transcription back with Whisper (`audio/verify_voice.py`) and WER against the script.
- BIG LESSON: with Whisper SMALL the WER came out 49–161% because it HALLUCINATED text in the silences and under the music ("Koch", "¡Claro! ¡Claro!"...). With Whisper LARGE-V3 (3.09 GB, `whisper-large-v3-asr-fp16`) there is no invented text and the WER is 0–2.4%. The only failure: the final "Arco" recognized as "Arcos". A small recognizer can rate a good audio as bad; distrust the metric before distrusting the audio. (The user suggested it.)
- Failed attempts: `whisper-small-mlx` (old format, mlx-audio does not load it: the processor is missing; the `-asr-fp16` variants must be used); the level measurement forgot the voice weight in amix (+4.6 dB) and underestimated the difference; an `asplit` with an unconnected output; my script hid the subprocess error.
- Honest limit: none of this measures whether the voice sounds NATURAL or pleasant, nor whether the music is good. That can only be judged by listening. I left two versions to choose from: `out/promo-hyperframes-sound-ef_dora.mp4` and `-em_alex.mp4`.
- `make audio` regenerates everything; `make audio-verify` repeats the checks.

- Context: the user asked whether Kokoro was the best model. Source: Artificial Analysis (Kokoro 82M: Elo 1061, 56th overall, 7th among 17 open-weight models). - FINDINGS (mlx-audio 0.5.9): (1) the `speed` parameter is IGNORED (same durations at 1.0 and 1.3). (2) The pace is very unstable: the same sentence lasts from 4.96 to 9.60 s depending on the seed; in the long ones the voice sounds continuous for 7.5–8.5 s: it reads at half speed, dragging sounds. One of 6 seeds stuttered ("hay dos... hay dos..."). For "Arco." one candidate lasted 12.66 s. (3) temperature=0.3 made it worse. (4) `max_tokens` cuts the sentence. (5) An instruction IN ENGLISH ("Speak at a brisk, natural narration pace...") stabilized it (4.6–5.8 s); in Spanish it improved less.
- Solution in `audio/narration.py --engine qwen3`: 5 candidates per sentence with fixed seeds (reproducible), each one is transcribed with Whisper large-v3 and the shortest one whose text matches the script is chosen; if it still does not fit, ffmpeg atempo up to 1.25×. The windows are widened by 0.6 s (`--extra-window`) because Qwen3 is slower than the script designed for Kokoro.
- Results: all sentences verified (0 errors); only `ryan` s3 needed 1.25×. Mixes: -16.0 and -16.6 LUFS, peak -1.5 dB; Whisper large: WER 0.0% in both full mixes. Voice over music: +17 to +31 LU (more than Kokoro: Qwen's voices come out louder and the ducking sinks them more).
- My false alarm: the measurement gave "+4.6 LU" for the closing because the "Arco." window (0.41 s) is shorter than the 0.4 s that ebur128 requires; it is now skipped with a notice.
- Costs: ~34 s per voice with Qwen3 (5 candidates x 5 sentences + Whisper verification) versus 3.8 s with Kokoro; 3.08 GB versus 0.39 GB.
- What I do NOT know: which sounds more natural, nor whether the accent of the non-native speakers is bothersome. That is for the user's ear to decide. Videos: `out/promo-hyperframes-sound-qwen3-ryan.mp4` and `-qwen3-aiden.mp4`; reference: `out/promo-final.mp4` (Kokoro em_alex).

## 2026-10-15 · Voice reproducibility (Kokoro)
- Finding: Kokoro is NOT deterministic. Two runs without a seed give different waveforms (SNR 7–13 dB; same duration and same recognized text). My previous entry saying otherwise was false (see the correction above).
- Fix: `mx.random.seed(0)` before each generation (`audio/narration.py`, constant SEED). Result: two runs differ in 16 of 480,000 samples, by 1/32767 at most (SNR 107 dB): reproducible up to rounding, not byte for byte (probably the order of operations on the GPU).
- Consequence: the regenerated voice has the same duration and the same words as the one the user chose, but a different realization of the internal noise. If the exact earlier version is wanted, it cannot be recovered (it had no seed).
- Qwen3 is not affected: it uses its own seeds per candidate.
